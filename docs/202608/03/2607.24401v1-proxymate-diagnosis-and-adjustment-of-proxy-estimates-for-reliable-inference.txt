## Abstract 

Proxy outcomes (such as short-term behavioral signals, model pre-dictions, or surrogate endpoints) are frequently used in place of primary outcomes that are too slow to mature, rare, or challeng-ing to measure directly. But valid inference on a proxy does not guarantee valid inference on the primary estimate as proxy-based estimates can be systematically biased in ways that are difficult to predict, leading to improperly calibrated confidence intervals. We present proxymate , a framework and open-source Python package for proxy validation and adjustment. proxymate organizes into four levels: The Representativity Level (population validity), the Unit Level (measurement quality), the Estimate Level (decision validity), and the Domain Level (cross-domain transportability). Within each level, proxymate provides diagnostic checks, and tar-geted adjustment strategies that map specific failures to appropriate corrections. At Meta, proxymate has been adopted by many different use cases, spanning experimentation, prevalence estimation, and mon-itoring use cases, all facing different proxy challenges (limited human review time, long maturation window of outcomes, low detectability) and showcasing the modularity of the framework. Across all products, proxymate assessed and corrected millions of proxy, primary unit comparisons. It has facilitated launches across multiple work streams including enabling quick decision making on thousands of experiments.

## 1 Introduction 

In many scientific and industrial settings, the primary outcome of interest is difficult to measure. Outcomes may be delayed (e.g., 60-day attribution [ 38 ]), require time consuming human labeling (e.g., identifying content that violates an online platform’s policies [ 29 ]), or be observable only for a small, non-representative fraction of the population. As a result, practitioners frequently substitute a more readily available proxy outcome and make decisions based on inference computed on that proxy. The use of proxies has been first explored, adopting the term surrogate endpoints, in medicine [ 10 , 34 ]. A surrogate in this con-text is a more readily available and perhaps less noisy outcome used in place of the primary outcome. When no single surrogate is sufficient, a surrogate index, a combination of weak surrogates may be constructed; more commonly used in social sciences where the presence of a single strong surrogate for the desired outcome may not exist [ 4 ]. Proxy metrics in online experimentation often serve a similar role [17]. Even when a proxy is strongly correlated with the primary out-come, proxy-based point estimates can be systematically biased for the primary estimand and their confidence intervals miscali-brated. Two failure modes recur in practice. First, unit-level diag-nostics computed on a labeled sample (calibration, accuracy, dis-crimination) can look adequate while the labeled sample itself is non-representative of the target population, so aggregate estimates on the full population are biased in ways the unit-level checks do not surface. Second, standard adjustment methods (e.g., Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) [ 2], regression calibration, isotonic recal-ibration) inherit the assumptions of the sample they are fit on: applied without first verifying representativity or cross-domain stability, they can leave the bias intact or amplify it. The gap is not the individual adjustment methods, but the absence of a diagnostic layer that determines whether and which adjustment is warranted before it is applied. Despite extensive methodological work, a persistent practical gap remains in effectively using proxy outcomes. Existing literature focuses on either adjustment (correcting a proxy’s flaws) or valida-tion (diagnosing whether a proxy is adequate, though the literature in this second area is more limited [ 44 ]), but rarely connects the two. Furthermore, most surrogate validation frameworks assume a controlled experimental setting with a specific treatment, mak-ing them inapplicable to generic measurement scenarios such as arXiv:2607.24401v1 [stat.ML] 27 Jul 2026 Conference’17, July 2017, Washington, DC, USA Darmon et al. 

population-level estimation or observational monitoring. No exist-ing framework spans unit-level quality, aggregate-level decision validity, and cross-domain transportability while also addressing the representativity of the labeled sample. This paper introduces proxymate 1, a framework and open-source Python package for proxy metric validation and adjustment. While proxymate builds on established diagnostic methods (e.g., SMD for balance [ 5], ECE for calibration [ 16 , 28 ], 𝐼 2 for heterogene-ity [ 19 ]) and established adjustment methods (e.g., PPI [ 2 ], Platt scaling [ 33 ], regression calibration [ 11 ]), the individual checks are not our contribution. Our contributions are the framework that connects them, the open-source package, and its application at Meta: (1) A four-level validation framework. We organize proxy validation and correction into four levels: the Representativ-ity Level (population validity), the Unit Level (measurement quality), the Estimate Level (decision validity), and the Do-main Level (cross-domain transportability). Each level corre-sponds to a distinct statistical question, data structure, and set of diagnostics and adjustments (Section 3). (2) A theoretical diagnostic-to-adjustment bridge. For each level, we map theoretically specific failure modes to the adjustment whose assumptions are satisfied, formally closing the gap between diagnosing a proxy problem and delivering a corrected proxy in production. (3) Empirical application at scale. Through multiple produc-tion applications of proxymate at Meta, we demonstrate its value in identifying proxy failures, choosing among candi-date proxies and performing guided adjustment. We share practical lessons about proxy adoptions or failures in pro-duction. (4) An open-source package . The package exposes modular validation checks, configurable planners, custom estimators for aggregate metrics such as Average Treatment Effects, and structured validation reports that can be consumed pro-grammatically or rendered for practitioners. At Meta, proxymate has been used across several production ap-plications spanning experimentation, prevalence monitoring, and ML model training (six of which have been detailed in Table 2). These applications collectively span four distinct primary-outcome bottlenecks (long maturation windows, restricted measurement, low detectability, low sensitivity), and exercise every component of the framework: Representativity Level validation in three, Unit Level in all six, Estimate Level in five, Domain Level in three, and Es-timate Level adjustment in one. In four applications, a validated (or adjusted-then-validated) proxy was used, enabling ongoing experi-ment volume of approximately 7,500 experiments annually across the four (dominated by ∼7,000 Long Attribution Window (LAW) conversion experiments and ∼300 Nonpayment experiments, with the remaining two applications accounting for the balance). In the remaining two, all candidate proxies were rejected, preventing use of a personalization model trained on biased outcomes and redirect-ing proxy development toward an ensemble approach. Across all products where proxymate was applied, it assessed and corrected millions of proxy–primary unit comparisons. Sections 4.3 and 4.4

## 1.1 Related Work 

Surrogate endpoints and proxy inference. The surrogate endpoint problem originates in biostatistics [ 9 , 10 , 34 ] and was extended to the social sciences via Athey et al.’s surrogate index [ 4 ] and Tripuraneni et al.’s proxy selection criteria [ 44 ]. proxymate comple-ments selection by validating a given proxy across multiple levels of aggregation. 

Prediction-powered inference and post-prediction adjustment. 

Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) [ 2, 3 ] debiases model-based estimates with valid CIs; Wang et al. [ 50 ] and Ji et al. [ 21 ] broaden this into post-prediction inference; Wilkins-Reeves et al. [ 52 ] model residual proxy bias as a cross-domain random effect. These are ad-justment mechanisms; proxymate provides the diagnostic layer that determines whether and which adjustment to apply, and we prove that PPI applied to an already-adequate proxy strictly in-creases MSE (Theorem 1). We also draw on established diagnostics from the calibration [ 16 , 28 , 33 ], covariate shift [ 5 , 40 ], and meta-analysis [14, 19] literature. 

Existing software. Causal-inference packages (DoWhy [ 39 ], EconML) focus on treatment effect estimation; ppi_py implements PPI without diagnostic checks; experimentation platforms (Growth-Book, Statsig) lack formal proxy validation. proxymate fills this gap with a unified diagnose-then-correct workflow across four levels. We present a more comprehensive discussion of the related fields of literature in Appendix D.

Proxy validation relies on the historical domains 𝑘 = 1, . . . , 𝐾 − 1,where both the primary outcome 𝑌 and the proxy outcome 𝑌 ∗ are observed. Within each historical domain 𝑘 , we refer to the data containing paired (𝑌, 𝑌 ∗, 𝑋 ) observations as the validation data

If there is a non-uniform missingness, a first step is required to weight the population with respect to the target population of interest. The representativity of V𝑘 within domain 𝑘 is a separate con-cern from whether the proxy-primary relationship generalizes from historical domains to the target domain 𝐾 . ## 3 The proxymate Framework 

Proxymate decomposes proxy diagnosis and adjustment into four levels. The first (Representativity) level addresses population valid-ity : whether the validation data on which all checks are computed should be reweighted before use. The remaining three levels ad-dress proxy quality at increasing levels of aggregation. Unit Level 

diagnoses whether the proxy tracks the primary at the individual 

level. Estimate Level diagnoses whether the proxy estimate gives the same aggregate answer as the primary estimate within a sin-gle domain. Domain Level diagnoses whether the proxy-primary relationship is stable across domains .Each level pairs a diagnostic question with the family of ad-justments whose assumptions the diagnostics test. Further, each level plays a distinct role: collapsing Unit and Estimate would ob-scure that individual errors can systematically compound in ag-gregation, while collapsing Estimate and Domain would conflate within-domain bias with cross-domain instability. 

Segment-level validation. A proxy that passes on the overall pop-ulation may fail for specific segments (subpopulations defined by conditioning on covariates within a single domain). Proxymate 

supports running all Unit and Estimate Level checks per segment, and segment-level results can be aggregated into a Domain Level 

meta-analysis to assess stability across subpopulations.

3.2.1 Diagnostics. The Unit Level evaluates six complementary properties of unit-level proxy quality. Accuracy: Does the proxy have a systematic bias? This is tested via Welch’s t-test [ 51 ] on 

E[𝑌 ∗ − 𝑌 ], with optional importance weights. Precision: How noisy is the proxy? This is measured by 𝑅 2, RMSE, and Pearson correlation [ 32 ]. Agreement: Do values agree on the identity line? This is measured using Bland-Altman [ 7], directional concordance and Lin’s Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) [ 26 ] which decomposes as CCC = 𝜌 ·𝐶 𝑏 , separating correlation from location/s-cale mismatch. Calibration: Do predicted probabilities match ob-served frequencies (essential for prevalence estimation)? This is measured by Expected Calibration Error (ECE) [ 28 ] and the Brier score [ 8]. Discrimination: Does the proxy correctly rank units? This is measured by Kendall’s 𝜏 [22 ] for continuous outcomes and Receiver Operating Characteristic - Area Under the Curve (ROC AUC) [ 18 ] for binary. Note that this is independent of calibration. 

Distributional fidelity: Do the marginal distributions match? This is measured by KS test [ 25 ], Total Variation (TV) distance, and Wasserstein-1 [ 48 ]. A complete list of the Unit Level checks can be found in Table 4 in Appendix A. 

3.2.2 Adjustments. When Unit Level diagnostics reveal failures, 

proxymate maps each failure mode to a targeted adjustment: 

• Systematic bias (accuracy failure): Additive bias adjustment subtracts the estimated mean difference E[𝑌 ∗−𝑌 ] from proxy scores. 

• Miscalibration (calibration failure): Platt scaling [ 33 ] fits a logistic regression P(𝑌 = 1 | 𝑌 ∗) = 𝑠 (𝑎𝑌 ∗ + 𝑏 ), where 

𝑠 (𝑧 ) = 1/( 1 + 𝑒 −𝑧 ) is the logistic sigmoid, appropriate when miscalibration is approximately monotone. Isotonic regres-sion [ 54 ] fits a non-parametric monotone function and is more flexible but requires a larger labeled sample. For prox-ies that must be calibrated across subgroups (e.g., different content types or user segments), multicalibration methods such as MCGrad [ 43 ] provide scalable post-hoc calibration that ensures calibration holds within data-driven subgroups, not just globally. 

• Poor discrimination : This cannot be fixed by post-hoc adjustment and requires proxy replacement or model re-training. 

## 3.3 Estimate Level : Decision Validity 

Does the proxy lead to the same aggregate conclusion as the pri-mary within a single domain? The Estimate Level assesses whether the proxy is reliable for aggregate inference (point estimates and confidence intervals) within a single domain. Even if a proxy is well-calibrated at the unit level, individual errors can compound or cancel in aggregation in unpredictable ways. A proxy that is noisy but unbiased at the unit level may produce reliable aggregate estimates; a proxy that is accurate on average may still produce bi-ased aggregates if its errors correlate with treatment assignment or covariates. When the Representativity Level has identified a gap, all aggregate estimates at this level are computed using the importance-weighted validation data V𝑘 .The Estimate Level checks are computed over 𝐵 bootstrapped samples from the domain. ## 4 Applying proxymate at Meta

## 4.1 System and Workflow 

proxymate is implemented as a modular Python library. All valida-tion checks inherit from a generic abstract base class parameterized by result and input types. New checks can be added by subclassing this base without modifying the orchestrator. The data handler provides a uniform interface for using in-memory data (Pandas dataframes). The comprehensive list of diagnostics available in the package is presented in Tables 3 - 6. The validation planner de-scribed in Section 3 (details in Appendix C.2) is implemented as a configurable routing table; practitioners can also select checks man-ually or programmatically. ## 4.4 Case Study B: Scam Prevalence 

Problem and setup. To estimate scam prevalence on a messaging platform, expert review (primary) is available only for a small la-beled sample drawn from a review queue, which over-represents flagged content and under-represents benign content, while an ML classifier (proxy) scores all content. The dataset has >1M target accounts; ∼42K have paired expert and proxy labels. We evaluate three proxy candidates: Proxy A (scaled human review), Proxy B and C (two LLM classifiers). This use case exercises the Representa-tivity , Unit , and Estimate Levels .

Results. The Representativity Level flags a data-quality problem. Proxy A passes sample representativity (SMD within bounds) but fails effective sample size: the IW weights are highly variable, in-dicating the labeled sample is concentrated in a small covariate region, so IW cannot fully recover population-level estimates (Ap-pendix B.8). At the Unit Level , Proxy A agrees strongly with expert review on the unweighted sample. After IW reweighting to the tar-get population, classification sensitivity (true positive rate) drops sharply: 17.3% for Proxy A, 31.6% for B, 30.1% for C. The proxies per-form well on over-represented flagged accounts but poorly on the broader population. None of the proxies pass Unit Level validation. At the Estimate Level , the naive expert-review prevalence is 31.9%; the IW-corrected estimate is 3.0% (roughly 11 × lower, best-available under covariate shift, not ground truth). PPI applied to Proxy A yields 10.7%, down 3.3 pp from its naive proxy estimate of 13.9%, still ∼3.5 × the IW-corrected primary. Unlike Case A, the aggregate checks do not rescue the proxy from its Unit Level failure, so the framework rejects all three candidates.

## 4.5 Learnings 

Applying proxymate across the applications at Meta (such as the ones in Table 2) surfaced several recurring patterns about how proxy validation operates in practice and where it delivers value. Validation requires paired proxy and primary signals jointly ob-served in the same population, but in practice the proxy is often not logged ahead of time, the primary matures only after a fresh cohort accrues, and the two frequently live in distinct data systems whose join keys must be reconstructed. Once assembled, the methodology itself runs as a single notebook execution. A second integration constraint shapes proxy selection: validated proxies must compose with the existing experimentation or reporting stack, and several applications accordingly chose a proxy that did not maximize statis-tical quality but fit cleanly into existing decision-rule infrastructure. This was an important production tradeoff: the best statistical proxy was not always the best deployable proxy. In multiple applications the validation layer’s primary contri-bution was to reject candidate proxies that would otherwise have entered downstream decision pipelines. Each rejection triggers a tractable next step, such as building a new proxy or changing the sampling design. Mature applications run proxymate repeatedly across succes-sive proxy metric versions or for ongoing monitoring. The data and sampling infrastructure assembled for the first pass should be treated as a persistent asset.

## 5 Conclusion 

We presented proxymate , a four-level framework for proxy esti-mate validation that spans population validity ( Representativity Level ), unit-level measurement quality ( Unit Level ), aggregate-level decision validity ( Estimate Level ), and cross-domain transportabil-ity ( Domain Level ). Beyond assembling existing diagnostics into a pipeline, the paper contributes: (i) the four-level framework itself, applicable uniformly to A/B surrogates, prevalence classifiers, and monitoring proxies; (ii) a diagnostic-to-adjustment bridge that maps each failure mode to a principled adjustment, formalised through Theorem 1, and Bridges B.1–B.7; and (iii) empirical validation across six production applications at Meta, with two case studies (long-term outcome prediction and prevalence estimation) demonstrating that proxymate identifies proxy failures that would otherwise lead to incorrect decisions. Lastly (iv) a modular open-source package for practitioners. 

Limitations. proxymate validates one proxy against one primary outcome at a time and establishes statistical agreement rather than causal mechanisms. Future directions include sequential validation via confidence sequences, multi-outcome validation, and automated proxy selection using proxymate diagnostics as an objective.

Proposition 6 (Monotone recalibration cannot improve rank discrimination). If ℎ is strictly monotone, then ROC AUC is invariant:

## B.8 When Weighting Fails to Correct Representativity Issues 

If the ESS is too low or the weighting effectiveness check fails, importance weighting alone cannot recover valid population-level estimates. proxymate flags this condition and recommends one of four fallback strategies: (1) Targeted label acquisition: acquire additional labels in under-represented regions of the covariate space to improve cover-age and reduce weight variability. (2) Narrow the target population: redefine the target 𝑇 to the proxy population 𝑃 (or the well-covered subset), accepting that validation results apply only to the narrower scope. (3) Sensitivity analysis: report bounds on the estimand under varying assumptions about the unobserved population, rather than a single point estimate. (4) Segment-restricted estimates: report validated estimates only for segments where the labeled sample is representative, with explicit scope limitations.

## C.3 Proxymate Levels: Diagnose-then-Adjust Loop 

At each level selected by the Planner, proxymate runs a two-step 

diagnose-then-adjust loop (Figure 1). The diagnose step evaluates the level’s checks and returns pass/fail verdicts. If all checks pass, no adjustment is applied and the level’s output is a clean verdict. If any check fails, the adjust step applies a targeted correction whose assumptions match the specific failure mode (Table 1), and the level is then re-validated on the corrected data. ## D Extended Related Work 

Surrogate endpoints and indices. The foundational work on proxy validation originates in biostatistics. Prentice [ 34 ] formalized the surrogate endpoint problem and proposed an operational criterion: a surrogate is valid if it fully captures the treatment effect on the primary outcome. This condition relies on strong causal assump-tions rarely satisfied in practice. Buyse and Molenberghs [ 9 , 10 ] ex-tended this to a meta-analytic framework, validating surrogates via 

𝑅 2

> trial

, the proportion of treatment effect variance explained by the surrogate across multiple trials. VanderWeele [ 46 ] distinguished be-tween statistical and causal surrogacy. In the social sciences, Athey et al. [ 4 ] introduced the surrogate index for combining multiple weak surrogates into a strong one, focusing on short-term outcomes as proxies for longer-term effects. proxymate complements these works by validating a given proxy across multiple levels of aggre-gation and aligning the validation criteria with the downstream application. 

Prediction-powered inference. Angelopoulos et al. [ 2] introduced Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI), which uses a small labeled dataset to correct the bias of a model evaluated on a large unlabeled dataset, providing valid confidence intervals. PPI++ [ 3] extends this with power-tuning for efficiency gains. Ji et al. [ 21 ] revisit the connection between predictions-as-surrogates and the classical surrogate outcome framework. Kluger et al. [ 24 ] extend PPI to set-tings with imputed covariates and nonuniform sampling, partially addressing representativity concerns. These methods are comple-mentary to proxymate : PPI provides an adjustment mechanism (used in our adjustment module), while proxymate provides the diagnostic layer that determines whether and which adjustment to apply. 

Calibration and post-hoc adjustment. Classifier calibration, ensur-ing predicted probabilities match empirical frequencies, is central to proxymate : Diagnosis and Adjustment of Proxy Estimates for Reliable Inference Conference’17, July 2017, Washington, DC, USA 

proxy quality at the unit level. Platt [ 33 ] introduced sigmoid scaling, while Zadrozny and Elkan [ 54 ] proposed histogram and isotonic cal-ibration. Guo et al. [ 16 ] demonstrated that modern neural networks are frequently miscalibrated and popularized temperature scaling. Naeini et al. [ 28 ] formalized the expected calibration error (ECE) as a standard diagnostic and [ 43 ] provides open source software for multicalibration. proxymate uses ECE and related calibration diagnostics at the Unit Level , and maps calibration failures to ap-propriate adjustment methods (Platt scaling, isotonic regression, or temperature scaling depending on model complexity and data availability). 

Estimate-level adjustment for proxy inference. Wilkins-Reeves et al. [ 52 ] introduce an estimate-level framework that models residual proxy bias as a random effect across domains. This adjustment inflates proxy-based confidence intervals to achieve calibrated cov-erage under random distribution shifts, using only aggregate sum-maries (no individual-level data required). Crucially, the adjustment is modular: it can be layered on top of any proxy adjustment method (including PPI), accounting for residual biases not addressed by those adjustments. proxymate integrates this estimate-level adjust-ment as an adjustment strategy for cross-domain transportability and for within-domain aggregate inference when historical domains are available. 

Proxy selection and learning. Zito et al. [ 55 ] propose Pareto-optimal proxy metrics that balance multiple criteria. Tripuraneni et al. [ 44 ] formalize proxy selection for experimentation, providing cri-teria for when a proxy is “good enough” to support decision-making. Bibaut et al. [ 6] study learning the covariance of treatment effects across many weak experiments. proxymate is complementary: it validates a given proxy rather than selecting among candidates, though its multi-level diagnostics can inform selection decisions and incorporate Tripuraneni’s criteria. 

Covariate shift and representativity. The covariate shift litera-ture [ 40 –42 ] addresses distribution mismatch between training and deployment populations via density ratio estimation and reweight-ing. Rosenbaum [ 36 ] and Austin [ 5 ] provide foundational treat-ments of propensity score methods and standardized mean dif-ferences (SMD) for assessing covariate balance. proxymate opera-tionalizes these ideas by integrating representativity diagnostics directly into the validation pipeline: before assessing proxy quality, we first verify that the labeled sample on which quality is evaluated is representative of the target population, using SMD and density ratio diagnostics to flag and optionally correct for selection bias. 

Prevalence estimation with classifiers. A body of work addresses population-level inference from classifier outputs. Rogan and Gladen [ 35 ] provided early methods for prevalence estimation using sensitivity and specificity. Forman [ 15 ] developed quantification learning methods that estimate class proportions without requiring calibrated individual predictions. Wu and Resnick [ 53 ] propose calibrate/extrapolate methods for prevalence estimation with black-box classifiers. Nguyen et al. [ 29 ] develop the CLARA framework for confidence of labels and raters for noisy label problems. These methods address specific instances of the proxy validation problem; 

proxymate provides the diagnostic scaffolding to determine which adjustment is appropriate in a given setting. 

Transportability and meta-analysis. Pearl and Bareinboim [ 31 ]formalize transportability as a binary verdict based on causal graph structure; proxymate replaces this with continuous diagnostics based on the heterogeneity machinery of DerSimonian and Laird [ 14 ](𝜏 2) and Higgins et al. [ 19 ] ( 𝐼 2). When heterogeneity is detected, 

proxymate applies the estimate-level adjustment of Wilkins-Reeves et al. [52] to inflate confidence intervals appropriately. 

Conformal prediction and distribution-free inference. Conformal prediction [ 49 ] and conformal risk control [ 1] provide distribution-free coverage guarantees under exchangeability. These methods construct prediction sets with guaranteed marginal coverage, which could complement proxy validation by providing unit-level uncer-tainty quantification. Measurement invariance testing from psy-chometrics assesses whether a measurement instrument functions equivalently across groups, conceptually related to our cross-domain transportability checks. proxymate does not currently integrate conformal methods but they represent a natural extension for distribution-free calibration diagnostics. 

Post-prediction inference. Wang et al. [ 50 ] formalized the “post-prediction inference” problem (using ML-predicted outcomes in downstream statistical analyses) and developed adjustment meth-ods based on modeling the relationship between predictions and true outcomes. Chen et al. [ 12 ] provide a unified framework for in-ference under general missingness patterns with machine learning imputation. Mozer [ 27 ] connects PPI to classical survey sampling estimators, showing that PPI is a difference estimator. These works address specific adjustment mechanisms; proxymate provides the diagnostic wrapper that determines when and how to apply them. 

Existing software. Despite the extensive literature, no existing Python package provides an end-to-end proxy validation workflow. Causal inference packages such as DoWhy [ 39 ] and EconML focus on treatment effect estimation, not proxy validation. The ppi_py 

package implements PPI but does not provide diagnostic checks. The ppi_aipw package [ 45 ] adds calibration to PPI but does not address representativity or multi-level validation. Experimentation platforms (GrowthBook, Statsig) offer metric monitoring but lack formal proxy validation. proxymate fills this gap by providing a unified diagnose-then-correct workflow spanning four levels of proxy quality.
